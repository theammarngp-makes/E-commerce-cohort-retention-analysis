# 15. Conclusion

## Summary
This analysis of 23 monthly cohorts (~95,560 customers, Sep 2016-Aug 2018) found that repeat purchasing is structurally rare: average month-1 retention across mature cohorts is just 0.33%, and no cohort-month ever exceeds 0.65%. This pattern held steady even as acquisition volume scaled roughly 8x between 2017 and 2018, indicating the business has been growing almost entirely through new-customer acquisition rather than repeat customers. This is a sustainability risk - CAC must currently be recovered almost entirely within the first order, since there is effectively no second-order revenue to amortize it against.

## Next Steps
- Feed these cohort findings into the Month-over-Month Growth Analysis (Project 4) to check whether topline growth is fully explained by new-customer acquisition, confirming the "growth is not retention-driven" hypothesis
- Prioritize and pilot the first-60-day re-engagement trigger (Recommendation 1) and measure its effect on the next 2-3 cohorts
- Longer-term: build a churn/repeat-purchase prediction model once a re-engagement program generates enough repeat-purchase examples to train on
