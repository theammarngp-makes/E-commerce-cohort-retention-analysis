# 01. Executive Summary

## One-Paragraph Summary
Across 23 monthly acquisition cohorts and ~95,560 customers (Sep 2016 - Aug 2018), repeat purchase behavior is almost nonexistent: on average, only 0.33% of a cohort returns to buy again in month 1, and retention never climbs meaningfully above ~0.6% in any single month post-acquisition. This is not a "retention decline" problem in the traditional SaaS sense — it is a near-total absence of repeat purchasing. The business is functioning almost entirely on new-customer acquisition, which is a structural risk to sustainable growth.

## Key Findings
- **Month-1 retention averages just 0.33%** across mature cohorts (Jan-Jun 2017), meaning roughly 1 in 300 customers returns within a month of their first order.
- **No cohort ever exceeds ~0.65% retention** in any single post-acquisition month — the August 2017 cohort had the best month-1 rate at 0.65%, still under 1%.
- **Retention decays further and flattens low** — by month 6, the average across mature cohorts is 0.32%; by month 13+, it drops to ~0.15%, with sporadic single-digit-customer spikes.
- **Cohort size grew substantially over time** (762 customers in Jan 2017 to 6,000-7,000+ per month by 2018), meaning the business scaled almost entirely through new-customer acquisition, not repeat customers.
- **2017 vs 2018 volume:** 43,468 customers acquired in 2017 vs. 51,792 in 2018 — growth is real, but not evidence of retention improving, since retention % stayed flat-to-low across both years.

## Business Impact
With retention this low, customer lifetime value is essentially capped at one order for the overwhelming majority of customers. Marketing/acquisition spend cannot be amortized over repeat purchases, meaning CAC must be recovered almost entirely within the first order's margin. Even a modest lift — say from 0.33% to 2% month-1 retention — would represent a 6x increase in repeat buyers from the existing customer base, at a fraction of the cost of acquiring new customers.

## Top Recommendation
Prioritize a **post-purchase re-engagement program** (email/discount trigger in the first 30-60 days) as the single highest-leverage intervention, since the data shows the business currently captures almost none of the available repeat-purchase opportunity — there is far more upside in lifting retention off a near-zero base than in optimizing an already-large acquisition funnel.
